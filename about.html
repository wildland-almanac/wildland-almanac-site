About & roadmap

The Wildland Almanac is an open, university-developed record of ecosystem change — built to be a durable public reference, updated every year, and extended over time toward CONUS coverage, benchmarking against established models, and forward-looking prediction.

DRAFT — roadmap items beyond the current release describe direction and intent, not committed dates. Near-term items are concrete; forward-looking items are clearly marked as exploratory.

What it is

The Wildland Almanac is a reconciled, multi-decade record of wildland ecosystem condition and change, derived from the Landsat archive at 30 m resolution. Nineteen biophysical properties — vegetation structure, carbon, water balance, fuels and fire behavior, disturbance, and drought vulnerability — are produced through a single, internally consistent pipeline so they can be read together as one stack rather than stitched from separate single-purpose products. California is the first full release; coverage of the contiguous United States is planned.

It is a data resource, not a software platform. The data live in one canonical place on Source Cooperative; this site documents what the properties mean, how to bring them into the tools you already use, and how they were made.

Provenance

Developed from UC Irvine Earth System Science research in collaboration with university scientists, and processed on UC Irvine computing infrastructure. The data are a University of California product, released openly under CC BY. Contact and feedback: mgoulden@uci.edu.

This dataset emerged from the Center for Ecosystem Climate Solutions (CECS) research program. Earlier work and publications referencing CECS share the same project lineage.

How it's made

All properties are derived from the Landsat record and a consistent set of training and reference data. The emphasis is on temporal and cross-property consistency — values that can be compared across years and across properties within a single release. Full methodology, per-layer, is in the documentation at the dataset root.

Vegetation & structure

Fractional cover and canopy height mapped with gradient-boosted models (XGBoost) on synthetic Landsat imagery built from COLD, trained on RAP, USFS, and ALS-derived (Allred et al. 2025) reference data.

Hydrology

Evapotranspiration, soil moisture, and runoff from regressions on AmeriFlux flux-tower observations, monthly Landsat, and PRISM meteorology, run through a monthly water balance.

Fire

Annual FARSITE-format landscape files assembled from LANDFIRE topography and predicted fuels/canopy variables, run through FlamMap under fixed weather to give relative, fuels-driven hazard.

Carbon & disturbance

Biomass from an independently produced hybrid of field plots and remote sensing, co-registered into the stack; productivity from NIRv and flux towers; disturbance severity from COLD-identified events.

Reconciliation is the point. Because the layers share inputs and a single pipeline — disturbance is computed as deltas in the cover and biomass layers, and all properties are co-registered to one grid — the properties are mutually consistent. That is what lets a single pixel's carbon, water, fire, and disturbance be read together.

Updated every year

Each annual release is a complete reprocessing of the full 1985-onward time series under one consistent code base, published as a new version directory (v2026.1, v2027.1, …). Water-year N data are released early in year N+1; older versions stay in place so published links and citations keep resolving.

Why full reprocessing. As methods improve, a given year's values can change between versions. Reprocessing the whole series each time keeps every release internally consistent — at the cost that you should use one version end-to-end and not splice years across versions. See The Data for the analysis implications.

Roadmap

The Almanac grows along two axes: coverage (more geography, more years, more properties) and capability (from a retrospective record toward benchmarking and prediction). The same architecture carries all of it — new outputs flow through the same pipes into the same tools.

Now · v2026.1

California, released

  • All 19 properties, water years 1985–2025, statewide at 30 m, on Source Cooperative.
  • Cloud-native (COG + STAC); streams directly into ArcGIS Pro, QGIS, and GDAL-based tools.
  • Open under CC BY, with full documentation at the dataset root.
Near term

Integration & CONUS

  • Worked integration guides and hosted layers for the Esri ecosystem (ArcGIS Online, Living Atlas) and demos for QGIS, Earth Engine, and Felt.
  • A streaming viewer for browsing the layers without any local setup.
  • CONUS coverage, beginning with decadal snapshots and extending toward the full record — the 1990 baseline is a particular emphasis, as most national products lack one.

Concrete and in progress — pacing depends on coordination and review cycles.

Direction · exploratory

Benchmarking against established models

  • Use the historical record to evaluate how widely-used forest and fire models (e.g. FVS, LANDIS) reproduce observed change — initialize from an early year, compare projections to what actually happened.
  • Publish the comparison openly, as reference content the field can build on.

A direction the multi-decade record uniquely enables — not yet a released result.

Direction · exploratory

Forward-looking prediction

  • Explore prediction of ecosystem trajectories built on the reconciled record — empirical what-ifs first, then physics-informed and machine-learning approaches.
  • Any predictive outputs would flow through the same pipes and be documented and validated like every other layer.

An aspiration the data positions well, contingent on collaboration and support. Stated as intent, not promise.

Where it sits

The Wildland Almanac belongs to the family of open, public-good geospatial data resources — not commercial platforms. The closest reference points, and what each is known for:

Hansen GFC
The canonical global forest-change record; published openly and adopted across conservation and agency work. The nearest analog in spirit.
NLCD
The institutionally mature template — a federally-homed, periodically-updated land-cover product embedded in agency workflows.
HydroSHEDS
A well-documented substrate-plus-guidance resource in hydrology, widely used and durably maintained.
LandTrendr
A methodology-and-data approach to change detection, diffused into the field through Earth Engine and academic adoption.

What distinguishes the Almanac within this family is the reconciliation of many properties into one consistent stack, the multi-decade depth, and the intent to pair the record with open benchmarking and, eventually, prediction.

Supporting the work

The Almanac is built and openly shared to support decision-making and scientific discovery. Sustaining a multi-decade public-good dataset — annual updates, a durable institutional home, expansion to CONUS — is an ongoing effort. Researchers, agencies, and funders interested in the data, in collaboration, or in supporting continued updates are warmly invited to get in touch: mgoulden@uci.edu.

Feedback from users — errors, use cases, requests for additional properties — is genuinely valuable and materially shapes future releases.
